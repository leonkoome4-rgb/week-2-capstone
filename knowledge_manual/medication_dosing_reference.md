# AfyaPlus Medication Dosing Reference (Partner Clinics)

> TRAINING EXAMPLE, NOT MEDICAL ADVICE. These figures are simplified for a fictional student
> project. Never use them to treat a real patient - always follow a licensed clinician.

## 1. How to use this reference

Doses are weight-based. Multiply the patient's weight in kilograms by the dose per kilogram,
then never exceed the maximum single dose. To convert a dose in mg to a liquid volume in mL,
divide the dose by the syrup concentration in mg per mL.

## 2. Paracetamol (fever and pain)

- Dose: 15 mg per kg per dose.
- Maximum single dose: 1,000 mg.
- Frequency: every 6 hours, no more than 4 doses in 24 hours.
- Paediatric syrup concentration: 120 mg per 5 mL, which is 24 mg per mL.

## 3. Ibuprofen (fever, pain and inflammation)

- Dose: 10 mg per kg per dose.
- Maximum single dose: 400 mg.
- Frequency: every 8 hours with food.
- Paediatric syrup concentration: 100 mg per 5 mL, which is 20 mg per mL.
- Do not give to children younger than 6 months or to patients with asthma made worse by
  NSAIDs.

## 4. Amoxicillin (bacterial infections, prescription only)

- Dose: 25 mg per kg per dose.
- Maximum single dose: 500 mg.
- Frequency: every 12 hours for 5 days.
- Paediatric suspension concentration: 250 mg per 5 mL, which is 50 mg per mL.

## 5. Body mass index (BMI) bands used at triage

- Below 18.5: underweight.
- 18.5 to 24.9: normal weight.
- 25.0 to 29.9: overweight.
- 30.0 and above: obese - refer to the chronic disease programme for screening.
